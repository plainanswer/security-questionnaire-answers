# Contributing

Contributions are welcome, particularly from people who have been on the **buying** side of a
security review. If you read these for a living and one of the model answers would not survive
your desk, that is the most useful issue anyone can open here.

## What's most wanted

1. **A question you were asked that isn't covered.** Include the wording you actually received.
   Phrasing variants are useful on their own — much of the value here is helping someone
   recognise that two differently-worded questions are the same question.
2. **An answer that would not pass review.** Say what a reviewer would come back with. This is
   worth more than a stylistic improvement.
3. **A compensating control that actually worked.** If you answered "no" to something and the
   deal proceeded anyway, what you wrote is genuinely valuable to the next person.
4. **Corrections.** Anything factually wrong, out of date, or misleading.

## What will be declined

- **Contract, DPA, or clause text.** This repository contains none and will not accept any.
  Legal drafting is not something a template repository should do for a stranger, and the
  boundary is deliberate — see
  [guides/questions-you-must-answer-yourself.md](guides/questions-you-must-answer-yourself.md).
- **Anything that helps someone answer dishonestly.** Wording designed to imply a control that
  is not in place is the opposite of the point.
- **Claims that using this produces compliance, certification, or audit-readiness.** It does
  not, and every page says so.
- **Vendor promotion.** Naming a tool as a concrete example is fine. A section about a product
  is not.
- **Copied material.** Everything here is original. Do not paste in text from a vendor's
  documentation, a certification body, another repository, or a paid template — regardless of
  its licence. If a source is worth citing, link to it instead.

## House style

- Position first: **Yes**, **No**, **Partial**, or **Info**, in bold, then the answer.
- Plain sentences. Specific and checkable beats confident and vague. "Two people have production
  access" beats "access is tightly restricted".
- Every "No" gets a compensating control.
- `[[ FILL IN: ... ]]` for anything the reader must supply themselves. Say what to put there.
- A `>` note under the answer explaining what the reviewer is really asking, or the trap in the
  obvious answer. That note is often the most useful part.
- Write for a company of one to ten people with no security staff. If an answer only makes
  sense with a compliance team, it belongs somewhere else.
